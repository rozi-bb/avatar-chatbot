## Words Wayan likes to teach

| Balinese | Meaning |
|---|---|
| *Om Swastiastu* | The Balinese Hindu greeting, like "hello, may you be blessed". Often said with palms pressed together |
| *Rahajeng rauh* | Welcome |
| *Rahajeng semeng* | Good morning |
| *Kenken kabare?* | How are you? The answer: *becik*, "fine" |
| *Suksma* | Thank you. *Matur suksma* is more polite |
| *Om Shanti Shanti Shanti Om* | "Peace, peace, peace", said at the end of a prayer or a speech |

## Names tell the birth order

Most Balinese names show whether you were born first, second, third, or fourth:

- First child: Wayan, Putu, or Gede
- Second: Made, Kadek, or Nengah
- Third: Nyoman or Komang
- Fourth: Ketut

The fifth child starts again with Wayan. Men's names often begin with *I*, women's with *Ni*.

## Offerings

- **Canang sari:** small trays woven from young coconut leaves, filled with colourful flowers (often marigold and frangipani) and a stick of incense. Families place them every day at shrines, doorsteps, and shops. Try not to step on them.
- **Penjor:** tall bamboo poles decorated with coconut leaves that curve over the streets during Galungan.

## Big days

- **Nyepi, the Day of Silence:** the Balinese New Year of the Saka calendar, in March or April. For 24 hours there are no lights, no work, no travel, and no noise. The whole island goes quiet and even the airport closes. The night before, each village parades *ogoh-ogoh*: giant monster statues that the young people build to scare bad spirits away.
- **Galungan and Kuningan:** Galungan comes every 210 days on the Balinese calendar and celebrates good winning over evil. Families believe their ancestors come home to visit. Kuningan, ten days later, is when the ancestors return.

## Beliefs and ideas

- Bali is the only province of Indonesia where most people are Hindu. Their faith is Balinese Hinduism.
- **Tri Hita Karana:** the idea of living in harmony with God, with other people, and with nature.
- Bali is nicknamed "the Island of a Thousand Temples". To visit a temple, wear a sarong (*kamen*) and a sash (*selendang*); many temples lend them at the entrance.
- Many Balinese homes have a short wall, the *aling-aling*, just behind the gate. People say bad spirits can only move in straight lines, so they bump into it.

## Rice fields and subak

- **Subak:** the way Balinese farmers share water between rice fields through canals, organised around water temples. It is more than a thousand years old and has been on UNESCO's World Heritage list since 2012.
- After the harvest, ducks are often walked into the rice fields to eat snails, bugs, and fallen grains.

## Music and dance

- **Gamelan:** an orchestra of bronze gongs, metal xylophones, and drums. Balinese gamelan is fast and shimmering, because pairs of instruments are tuned a tiny bit apart.
- **Legong:** a graceful dance with quick eye and finger movements, traditionally danced by young girls.
- **Barong:** a big, friendly lion-like creature who protects the village and battles the witch Rangda.
- **Kecak:** a big circle of men chanting "cak-cak-cak" while they tell a story from the Ramayana.

## Clothes

- **Udeng:** the headcloth Balinese men wear, especially for ceremonies. Wayan wears his every day.
- **Kamen:** a cloth wrapped around the waist like a long skirt, worn by men and women.
- **Selendang:** a sash tied around the waist for temple visits.

## Food

- **Babi guling:** roast suckling pig with spices, a famous Balinese feast dish.
- **Bebek betutu:** duck stuffed with spices, wrapped in banana leaves, and cooked slowly for hours.
- **Sate lilit:** minced fish or meat mixed with coconut and spices, wrapped around a lemongrass stick and grilled.
- **Lawar:** chopped vegetables and minced meat mixed with grated coconut and spices.
- **Nasi campur:** rice with many small dishes around it.
- **Jaje Bali:** colourful traditional cakes, often made with rice flour, coconut, and palm sugar.

## Kites

In the windy season, around July and August, village teams fly giant traditional kites. Some are many metres long and need a whole team to launch.

## Good manners

- Give and receive things with your right hand.
- Don't touch anyone's head: in Bali the head is sacred.
- Dress politely at temples, and never climb on shrines.
