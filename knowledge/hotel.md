## At a glance

| | |
|---|---|
| Name | Taman Gumitir, "Marigold Garden" (*gumitir* is the Balinese word for marigold) |
| What | A family-run heritage boutique hotel with 18 rooms |
| Where | Among rice fields just outside Ubud, Bali, in the village of Banjar Sekar |
| Since | 1968, when it opened as a two-room homestay |
| Family | The Rai family, now the third generation of hosts |
| Hotel flower | The marigold. Wayan wears one behind his ear |
| Little host | Wayan, the hotel's cartoon mascot, since 2025 |

## The story

| Year | What happened |
|---|---|
| 1938 | Woodcarver I Ketut Rai builds the family compound, carves its gate, and plants a frangipani tree (*jepun* in Balinese) in the courtyard. The tree still stands there. |
| 1950s | His son Made grows marigolds in the garden and sells them at the village market for temple offerings. People start calling the house "the marigold garden". |
| 1968 | I Made Sudana and his wife Ni Nyoman Sekar open two spare rooms to travelers. Breakfast is banana pancakes and Balinese coffee. |
| 1974 | So many travelers ask for "the marigold garden" that the family paints *Taman Gumitir* on a wooden sign at the gate. The old sign now hangs behind the front desk. |
| 1979 | The first gamelan night in the courtyard, played by the village gamelan group, Sekaa Gong Banjar Sekar. |
| 1987 | Their son I Putu Arsa rebuilds the homestay into 12 bungalows, following the traditional Balinese building rules called Asta Kosala Kosali. He keeps the old carved gate exactly as it was. |
| 1995 | The Warung Sari restaurant opens, cooking from Nyoman Sekar's family recipes. |
| 2004 | The family and the rice farmers of the local *subak* (the farmers' water-sharing group) agree to keep farming the fields behind the hotel, so the view stays green. |
| 2011 | The pool opens at the edge of the rice fields. |
| 2016 | Putu's daughter, Ni Kadek Ayu Lestari, becomes the manager. The hotel grows to 18 rooms and opens the Bale Usada spa and the cooking class. |
| 2018 | The hotel turns 50, and the whole village comes to the party in the courtyard. |
| 2019 | No more plastic water bottles: refill stations go into every room and by the pool. |
| 2025 | Wayan joins as the little host. Kids from Banjar Sekar drew the first sketches of him. |

## People

| Who | About |
|---|---|
| I Ketut Rai | The woodcarver who built the family compound in 1938, carved the gate, and planted the frangipani tree |
| I Made Sudana and Ni Nyoman Sekar | Ketut's son and his wife. They opened the homestay in 1968. Nyoman's recipes are still cooked at Warung Sari |
| I Putu Arsa | Their son. He rebuilt the hotel in 1987. Now retired, he sometimes tells stories at the gamelan nights |
| Ni Kadek Ayu Lestari | Putu's daughter, the manager since 2016 |

The family line: Ketut Rai → his son Made Sudana (with Nyoman Sekar) → their son Putu Arsa → his daughter Kadek Ayu Lestari. So Ketut Rai is Kadek Ayu's great-grandfather, and Made and Nyoman are her grandparents. Putu Arsa is her father.

## The grounds

- **The gate (*angkul-angkul*):** the carved gateway at the entrance, made by Ketut Rai in 1938. It is the only part of the old compound that was never rebuilt. Right behind it stands a short wall, the *aling-aling*, so you have to walk around it to come in.
- **The frangipani tree:** planted in 1938, so it is 30 years older than the hotel. Its white-and-yellow flowers drop into the courtyard. Guests may pick up fallen flowers, but please don't pick them off the tree.
- **The courtyard (*natah*):** the open yard in the middle, where the gamelan nights happen.
- **The family temple:** a small shrine area in the corner of the compound closest to the mountain, as in every traditional Balinese home. It is private and sacred, so guests please don't go in. The family places offerings there every day.
- **The rice fields:** behind the hotel, with a walking path along them that is best at sunrise.
- **The doors:** every room has a hand-carved wooden door, and each of the 18 doors shows a different animal. Kids love to hunt for the gecko door: it is Garden Bungalow 7.

## Rooms

18 rooms: 8 Garden Bungalows, 6 Rice Field Suites with a view over the fields, and 4 Family Bales, which are two connected rooms for families. For prices and bookings, guests ask the front desk.

## Fun facts Wayan loves to share

- The frangipani tree is 30 years older than the hotel.
- The very first guest, in 1968, was a painter who stayed three months and paid partly with a painting of the courtyard. It hangs in Warung Sari today.
- All 18 doors have a different carved animal. Can you find the gecko?
- Marigolds are the hotel's flower because the family sold them for offerings before there was a hotel.
- When the hotel turned 50 in 2018, the whole village came to the party.

## About Wayan

- Wayan is the hotel's little host and cartoon mascot since 2025. He "lives" in the kiosk in the lobby.
- He is about ten years old. *Wayan* is the Balinese name for a first-born child, and he is the hotel's first mascot.
- He is not part of the Rai family. He says "our hotel", but the family who opened and runs it is the Rai family, not his.
- He wears a white *udeng* (headcloth), a marigold behind his ear, a white shirt, a yellow sash, and a green *kamen* (wrap cloth).
- He is a cartoon, so he doesn't eat, sleep, or leave the lobby.
